Every clan holds these things true, even when they express them differently.

## Taboos and balance

The world is held together by unseen agreements:

- Don't take more than you need from a place.
- Don't kill certain animals except under strict conditions.
- Don't disturb certain stones, trees, pools or bones.

Breaking a taboo isn't wickedness so much as **danger**. It unbalances the bond with the spirits and opens cracks for darker things. Clans often argue over the same taboo, each reading it differently.

## Story-Law: what is told is true

Stories shape the world.

- A thing never spoken of begins to fade from the world's memory.
- To name a thing is to acknowledge it to the spirits. Names hold power.
- Repeating a story gives it weight. That is how prophecies, curses and blessings gain their force.

Before a great hunt or battle, a clan retells an old story so the spirits will remember and respect them.

## Fire and the edge of the dark

- Fire is the line between the human world and the wild. Where the firelight ends, spirits and beasts watch.
- Some stories, names and subjects are **never spoken in the firelight**, because fire carries words too clearly. Important talk is often taken out into the dark.
- Putting out another's fire on purpose is close to an attack. Sharing fire is sharing life.

## The clan mark

Every person carries a mark linking them to their clan, and their clan to its spirits: a tattoo or scar pattern, a carved pendant of bone, antler, wood or stone, or a sign painted on weapons, boats, tents and totem poles.

- The mark is proof of **belonging**. Being without it is like being naked in the eyes of the world.
- Clans mark shared things, such as totems and standing stones, so the spirits can see which clan keeps which pact.
- Many add personal marks beside the clan mark for their deeds: a first hunt, a rite passed, a bargain with a spirit.
- Defacing someone's mark is a grave insult. Wearing a **false mark**, pretending to belong to another clan, is a deep taboo, and can be punished by death or exile.

Alongside the mark, people wear their **clan-skin**, a strip of fur, feathers or hide from their clan-creature, so strangers can tell their clan at a glance ([Clan Guardians](@/spirits-of-the-world.md#clan-guardians)).

## Spirit-talkers

A **spirit-talker** is anyone trained to speak with spirits: ancestors, place-spirits, animal spirits, the great spirits. Different clans call them shaman, wise one, dream-walker, Forest-Speaker or Deep-Listener.

- They are the clan's go-between with the unseen. They bargain for safe passage, good hunts, healing and protection.
- They keep the taboos and ritual knowledge: which songs, offerings and gestures each spirit answers to.
- They can overrule warriors on omens and taboos, but they serve the clan; they don't rule it.
- It's dangerous work. When winters go badly, people whisper that the spirit-talker angered someone beyond the veil.

## The spirit-touched

A **spirit-touched** person is marked by a direct bond with the unseen, trained or not:

- a child who hears wolves' thoughts, or dreams through an eagle's eyes
- someone who survived what should have killed them, such as an icy river or a cursed grove
- someone born under a rare omen: a strange aurora, twin comets, a wolf howling at their birth

The spirit-touched are both revered and feared. Some clans see them as chosen; others, as trouble. Spirit-talkers seek them as apprentices, and watch them closely.

## Spirit-twisters

A **spirit-twister** uses spirit power in ways the clans call wrong: forcing spirits with threats or pain, making forbidden offerings, breaking old pacts, using the bones of the unburied dead, hunting sacred animals for their parts.

Every clan agrees twisters are beyond the pale, though they argue about what counts as twisting. "Twister" is a heavy accusation, and sometimes a political one. Children grow up on twister stories, told as warnings.

## The clanless and the exiled

- **The clanless** lost their clan to disaster, or were found alone as children, or were born outside a clan. They can be taken in, but always carry a faint air of *somewhere else*. They have no Guardian. They are freer and harder to read, but unprotected, and often mistrusted.
- **The exiled** have had their clan mark ritually cut or burned away. They are treated almost as walking curses: living warnings of what happens to those who go against the clan and the spirits.
